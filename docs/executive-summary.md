# Reader's Way — Executive Summary

**Status:** living document, written 2026-07-03 after the research review (bead `rw-jvh`)
**Audience:** non-technical — pilot participants, educators, advisors

**What it is.** Reader's Way is a reading-practice app for children in Kindergarten
and 1st grade, built for ten calm minutes a day with a parent sitting alongside. It
teaches the foundations of reading the way the research says they're best taught:
step by step, sound by sound, with lots of short, spaced-out practice — and none of
the points, streaks, or cartoon rewards that most children's apps use, because the
evidence says those actually get in the way of learning.

**How it works.** A parent signs in with a link sent to their email — no password to
remember. They add their child, pick Kindergarten or 1st grade, and tap "Start
practice." The app hands them a short daily set — about 16 cards for a
kindergartner, 22 for a first grader. The child reads each card aloud — a sound, a
word, or a short sentence — and the parent simply taps "got it" or "try again."
That's the whole routine. A first grader doesn't start from scratch: they get a
quick review of the kindergarten foundations that speeds up as soon as they show
they know it.

**What's behind the cards.** The app quietly does the teacher-planning work: it
tracks which sounds and words the child has mastered, brings back the shaky ones at
just the right moment, mixes in review so nothing fades, and only introduces a new
skill when its building blocks are solid. Anything the child misses comes back
tomorrow — never as a punishment today. Sentences only ever use words the child has
actually been taught to sound out, so reading is decoding, never guessing.

**How progress shows up.** Parents see a simple progress view: what's been
practiced, what's mastered, where the sticking points are. The child's reward is the
real one — the growing feeling of "I can read this" — marked by one calm
acknowledgment when something is truly mastered, not confetti.

**Where it stands.** The app is in a private family pilot phase this summer, with a
small group of trusted educators and their families invited next — after the
recorded letter-sound audio is reviewed by a speech-language pathologist, and the
public-facing pages (landing, privacy, terms) are in place. It's free during the
pilot, collects only the minimal practice data needed to improve it, shows no ads,
and sells nothing.

**In one sentence:** it's a thoughtful daily reading workbook that plans itself —
short, structured, evidence-based practice that helps young readers build calm
confidence with a caring adult nearby.
